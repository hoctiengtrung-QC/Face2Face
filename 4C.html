The world's weirdest museums
Matthew Clifford visits some of the strangest museums on the planet
Museum of Bad Art — Massachusetts, USA
Have you ever wandered around a famous art gallery looking at paintings by well-known artists and secretly thought they were dreadful? Well, perhaps you should check out the Museum of Bad Art. It's the only museum in the world that celebrates bad art in all its forms. From blue-faced people to surreal landscapes, nothing is too bizarre for this museum.
Marikina Shoe Museum — Philippines
If fashion is more your thing, you might want to visit the Marikina Shoe Museum. The highlight of this museum is Imelda Marcos's massive shoe collection. When her husband was President she famously owned over 3,000 pairs of shoes, and 749 pairs are now displayed in the museum.
Cancun Underwater Museum — Mexico
Another unusual day out is the Cancun Underwater Museum. It was created by an English artist named Jason deCaires Taylor, who placed about 300 life-size concrete sculptures of real people on the seabed. You need to be able to dive or snorkel to explore the museum. The sight of brightly-coloured tropical fish swimming between the sculptures, which appear to be frozen in time, is absolutely breathtaking.
Miniatur Wunderland — Hamburg, Germany
In the days before video games, boys used to spend their free time playing with toy trains. Take your grandad to visit the largest miniature railway museum in the world. It has 13,000 metres of track, 930 trains with over 14,000 carriages, 300,000 lights, 228,000 trees and 215,000 human figures. It's an impressive sight — and they haven't finished building it yet!
Hamburger Museum — Daytona Beach, Florida
Are you feeling a bit peckish? If so, the Hamburger Museum is the place for you. There are burger-shaped biscuit jars, clocks, hats, music boxes, pencil holders, children's toys, and much more. The museum was set up by a man called Hamburger Harry, who has collected over 1,000 burger-related items.
