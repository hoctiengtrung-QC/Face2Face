📖 Newspaper/Magazine Article — Student Book p34–35
The world's weirdest museums
If you're bored of traditional museums, then perhaps it's time to try something new. Matthew Clifford visits some of the strangest museums on the planet.

Have you ever wandered around a famous art gallery looking at paintings by well-known artists and secretly thought they were dreadful? Well, perhaps you should check out the Museum of Bad Art in Massachusetts, USA. It's the only museum in the world that celebrates bad art in all its forms. From blue-faced people to surreal landscapes, nothing is too bizarre for this museum (which you can also visit online).

If fashion is more your thing, then you might want to visit the Marikina Shoe Museum in the Philippines. The highlight of this museum is Imelda Marcos's massive shoe collection. When her husband was President she famously owned over 3,000 pairs of shoes, and 749 pairs are now displayed in the museum. Oh, and there's also a giant high-heeled shoe you can drive around like a car.

Another unusual day out is the Cancún Underwater Museum off the coast of Mexico. It was created by an English artist named Jason deCaires Taylor, who placed about 300 life-size concrete sculptures of real people on the seabed. You need to be able to dive or snorkel to explore the museum, or you can hire a glass-bottomed boat. The sight of brightly-coloured tropical fish swimming between the sculptures, which appear to be frozen in time, is absolutely breathtaking.

In the days before video games, boys used to spend their free time playing with toy trains. Ask your grandad – or better still, take him to visit the largest miniature railway museum in the world. The Miniatur Wunderland in Hamburg, Germany, is huge and tiny at the same time. It has 13,000 metres of track, 930 trains with over 14,000 carriages, 300,000 lights, 228,000 trees and 215,000 human figures. It's an impressive sight – and they haven't finished building it yet!

Are you feeling a bit peckish? If so, perhaps the Hamburger Museum at Daytona Beach, Florida, is the place for you. There are burger-shaped biscuit jars, clocks, hats, music boxes, pencil holders, children's toys, and much more. The museum was set up by a man called Hamburger Harry, who has collected over 1,000 burger-related items. The best exhibit is probably the bed that's shaped like a giant cheeseburger – or perhaps it's the hamburger-shaped Harley-Davidson motorbike!

📚 Help with Vocabulary: Guessing meaning from context

🎯 Kỹ năng: Khi đọc, bạn không cần biết nghĩa của mọi từ. Hãy dùng văn cảnh xung quanh (câu trước/sau, loại từ, ví dụ) để đoán nghĩa hợp lý.
3a-bTừ màu xanh (blue) trong bài — chọn nghĩa đúng
Nhìn vào các từ màu xanh trong bài đọc. Chúng thuộc loại từ gì? Chọn nghĩa đúng (a hoặc b) dựa vào văn cảnh.
1. wandered — "Have you ever wandered around a famous art gallery..."
a) walked around slowly
b) walked around quickly
✅ a) walked around slowly — khi đi xem tranh trong gallery, người ta thường đi chậm để ngắm nhìn.
2. well-known — "...looking at paintings by well-known artists..."
a) common
b) famous
✅ b) famous — "well-known" nghĩa là nổi tiếng, được nhiều người biết đến.
3. bizarre — "nothing is too bizarre for this museum"
a) expensive
b) strange
✅ b) strange — văn cảnh nói về "bad art" với "blue-faced people, surreal landscapes" → kỳ lạ/bizarre.
4. highlight — "The highlight of this museum is Imelda Marcos's shoe collection"
a) most interesting part
b) biggest part
✅ a) most interesting part — "highlight" thường có nghĩa là điểm nổi bật/đáng chú ý nhất.
5. appear — "the sculptures, which appear to be frozen in time"
a) want
b) seem
✅ b) seem — "appear to be" = trông có vẻ là / dường như là.
6. carriages — "930 trains with over 14,000 carriages"
a) parts of a train that people travel in
b) the people who drive trains
✅ a) parts of a train that people travel in — toa tàu (carriage = toa xe lửa).
7. peckish — "Are you feeling a bit peckish?"
a) a bit thirsty
b) a bit hungry
✅ b) a bit hungry — gợi ý ngay sau là về Hamburger Museum → liên quan đến đói bụng.
8. set up — "The museum was set up by a man called Hamburger Harry"
a) started
b) bought
✅ a) started — "set up" nghĩa là thành lập, khởi tạo.

📄 Xem/ẩn toàn bộ Audio Script (CD1·36) ›

PRESENTER: Good morning, and welcome to the programme. Today we're looking at free things to do in London — because to a lot of people, London appears to be a very expensive city to visit. But there are actually quite a lot of things you can do in the city for free.

PRESENTER: Let's start with museums and art galleries. Most of London's major museums, like the British Museum, the Natural History Museum and the Tate Modern, are completely free to enter. You only pay for special exhibitions. So that's a great way to spend a day without spending any money at all.

PRESENTER: Next, let's talk about downloadable walking tours. There are some excellent free apps you can download onto your phone that guide you around different areas of the city — they tell you the history of buildings as you walk past them. My favourite is the tour of the area around London Bridge. You don't need a map because the app uses GPS to guide you.

PRESENTER: If you're interested in nature, why not visit one of London's city farms? There are several across the city where you can see real farm animals — pigs, goats, chickens — completely free. Some of them even let you feed the animals, although you should always check the rules first.

PRESENTER: For music lovers, did you know you can hear free music at lunchtime? Several churches in the city centre host free lunchtime concerts — usually classical music, and definitely not opera, but they're a wonderful way to spend your lunch break.

PRESENTER: If politics interests you, the Houses of Parliament offer free tours, although you need to book in advance through your local MP, or you can simply watch a debate from the public gallery for free — no booking needed for that.

PRESENTER: And for something a bit different, why not watch a trial at the Old Bailey? The public can watch most criminal trials for free, although you do need photo ID to get in, and you can't take bags or phones into the court room.

PRESENTER: And finally, if you want to see how TV and radio programmes are made, several broadcasters offer free tickets to watch shows being recorded live in the studio. You can usually book these tickets online, and it's a fascinating experience.

PRESENTER: So there you go — eight great ways to enjoy London without spending a penny!
