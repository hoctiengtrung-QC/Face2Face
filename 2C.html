Sleepless nights: are you getting enough sleep?
Adapted from a newspaper article · face2face Intermediate 2nd edition, Cambridge University Press

Most experts agree that adults need around seven to nine hours of sleep a night. But according to recent research, many people in the modern world are getting far less than this. In fact, sleep experts say that we are in the middle of a "sleep epidemic" — a situation where huge numbers of people are not sleeping enough.

Why don't we sleep enough?

There are many reasons why people don't get enough sleep. Stress and worry are two of the most common causes — many people lie awake at night thinking about problems at work or at home. The modern 24-hour lifestyle is also a factor: people stay up late watching television, using computers, or socialising. Shift workers often have to work at night, which disrupts their natural sleeping patterns. New parents regularly suffer from sleep deprivation because their babies wake up several times in the night.

What are the effects of poor sleep?

Not sleeping enough can have serious effects on our health and wellbeing. In the short term, lack of sleep makes us feel tired and irritable. It also affects our ability to concentrate and make decisions. In fact, research shows that someone who hasn't slept for 17–19 hours performs as badly as someone who is over the legal drink-drive limit. In the long term, regularly not getting enough sleep is linked to serious health problems such as heart disease, obesity, and depression.

"People often think they can catch up on sleep at the weekend, but this doesn't really work. You can't make up for lost sleep in the way that you can make up for lost food." — Professor Jim Horne, Loughborough University
How can we improve our sleep?

There are many things we can do to improve the quality of our sleep. Experts suggest that we should keep to a regular sleep schedule — going to bed and getting up at the same time every day. We should avoid caffeine and alcohol in the evening, and try to relax before going to bed. Exercise is generally good for sleep, but not immediately before bedtime. Perhaps the most important piece of advice is to switch off all electronic devices — including phones and laptops — at least an hour before bed, as the blue light from screens interferes with our natural sleep hormones.

For those who suffer from serious insomnia, there are various treatments available, from relaxation therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) to, in some cases, medication. However, most experts agree that medication should only be used as a last resort, as it can sometimes make sleep problems worse in the long run.

How much sleep is enough?

Interestingly, some people seem to need very little sleep and can function perfectly well on just five or six hours a night. Scientists believe this is partly genetic. However, most of us cannot get away with this — for the majority of adults, less than seven hours a night leads to measurable impairments in health and performance. The bottom line? Make sleep a priority, not an afterthought.

⚙ Help with Grammar
Gradable & Strong Adjectives + Adverbs
1. Gradable Adjectives
Tính từ gradable (thông thường) mô tả những mức độ có thể tăng giảm. Dùng kèm với:
very · quite · rather · pretty · extremely · a bit · fairly
I'm very tired. / She was quite cold. / It was rather boring.
He was extremely angry. / I'm a bit frightened.
2. Strong / Extreme Adjectives
Tính từ strong/extreme đã mang nghĩa cực độ (không cần "very"). Dùng kèm:
absolutely · completely · utterly · totally · really
I'm absolutely exhausted. / She was completely terrified.
It was utterly hilarious. / He was totally furious.
❌ I'm very exhausted.  |  ❌ It was very freezing.
✅ I'm absolutely exhausted.  |  ✅ It was absolutely freezing.
3. "Really" — dùng được cả hai
really là từ đặc biệt — có thể dùng với cả gradable và strong adjectives:
It was really cold. ✅ (gradable)
It was really freezing. ✅ (strong)
📌 Tóm tắt:
• Gradable + very / quite / extremely / rather / pretty / a bit / fairly
• Strong + absolutely / completely / utterly / totally / really
• really dùng được cả hai loại

📌 Nguyên tắc:
• Weak forms thường xảy ra với các từ chức năng (function words): articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, conjunctions.
• Các từ nội dung (content words: nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) thường được phát âm ở strong form.
• Khi một từ được nhấn mạnh hoặc đứng cuối câu, ta dùng strong form: "Can you?" "Yes, I CAN."

›
→ Cả hai đều là weak form /həv/ — "have to" trong câu không được nhấn mạnh.
