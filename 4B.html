PAUL: So, Josie, what's your new TV series about?

JOSIE: Well, the first episode is about a British explorer called Ed Stafford. He's famous for being the first person to walk the entire length of the Amazon River.

PAUL: Wow, how long did that take?

JOSIE: It took him two and a half years! He started in Peru in 2008 and finished in Brazil in 2010 — he walked about 6,000 miles in total.

PAUL: That's incredible. What kind of problems did he have on the journey?

JOSIE: So many! He had to deal with insects all the time — ants, wasps, even scorpion stings. He also had problems with local tribes who were suspicious of him at first, and he had to cross through some really dangerous jungle areas controlled by drug traffickers.

PAUL: That sounds terrifying. Did he do it completely alone?

JOSIE: Actually, no — interesting story. He originally had a different guide, called Luke Collyer, but Luke had to leave after about a year because of personal reasons. He'd gone back to the UK. So Ed continued with a new local guide called Cho.

PAUL: How did his friends and family know where he was?

JOSIE: He used a satellite phone and he updated a blog regularly so people could follow his journey online. He also calculated that he'd walked about 6,000 miles by the time he reached the end!

PAUL: What did he do when he got home?

JOSIE: Apparently the first thing he did was have a hot shower and eat a huge meal — he'd lost a lot of weight during the expedition. He said he also slept for almost 27 hours straight!

PAUL: Amazing story. I think viewers are going to love this episode.

BIG RIVER MAN
What is his connection to Ed Stafford?

Martin Strel – or Big River Man, as he is often called – is a long-distance swimmer from Slovenia. Martin taught himself to swim when he was six, and in 1997 he became the first man to swim from Africa to Europe by crossing the Mediterranean Sea. Seven people had already tried to complete this swim without success.

In 2002 Strel swam the length of the Mississippi, which took him 68 days. The year before that he broke the world non-stop record by swimming for 84 hours and 10 minutes without stopping. But he's probably best known as the man who swam the length of the Amazon in 2007, something nobody had ever done before. He travelled 5,268 km and swam for over 10 hours a day. By the time he arrived at Belém, in Brazil, he had been in the water for 66 days. He now runs an adventure swimming company with his son in Slovenia.

⚙ Help with Grammar: Past Perfect
Student Book p32–33 · Grammar 4.3
⚙ Help with Grammar
Past Perfect — had + past participle
Khi nào dùng Past Perfect?
Khi có hai hành động trong quá khứ, ta dùng Past Perfect cho hành động xảy ra TRƯỚC, và Past Simple cho hành động xảy ra SAU.
Cho joined the expedition after Luke had gone back to the UK.
(1st: Luke had gone back → 2nd: Cho joined)

He also read messages that people had sent him.
(1st: people had sent messages → 2nd: he read them)

Ed calculated that he'd walked about 6,000 miles.
(1st: he had walked → 2nd: he calculated)
So sánh ý nghĩa
1. When I turned on the TV, the programme started. → chương trình bắt đầu SAU KHI tôi mở TV (đồng thời/ngay sau)
2. When I turned on the TV, the programme had started. → chương trình đã bắt đầu TRƯỚC KHI tôi mở TV (tôi vào muộn)
Cấu trúc
POSITIVE: subject + had (hoặc 'd) + past participle
→ I had finished. / I'd finished.

NEGATIVE: subject + hadn't + past participle
→ I hadn't finished.

📌 Quy tắc:
• had trong Past Perfect thường được phát âm yếu /əd/ và thường được rút gọn thành 'd: he'd gone, I'd finished.
• to trước nguyên âm/danh từ cũng thường ở weak form /tə/.
• Trọng âm rơi vào động từ chính (GONE, JOINED, EXPEDITION) — không rơi vào "had" hoặc "to".
